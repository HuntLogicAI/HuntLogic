Use this pack for Illinois whitetail strategy, NR lottery questions, "is IL worth applying" for trophy deer.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- IL DNR Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual Illinois Hunting and Trapping Digest is the canonical reference.
- IL Deer Hunting page lists current NR allocation and CWD zone map.

Why Illinois matters for whitetail:
- West-central IL (Pike, Adams, Brown, Schuyler, Calhoun counties) produces some of the largest whitetails in North America.
- Saturday/Sunday weekend firearm seasons — short windows, heavy pressure.
- Long archery season (October-January) is the preferred NR strategy for mature bucks.

NR draw mechanics:
- Archery: NR can buy a tag OTC after the resident application window — typically available in early August.
- Firearm: NR firearm tags via lottery, very limited allocation, multi-year wait for popular counties.
- Total NR allocation has been gradually shrinking — verify current year.

Tag structure:
- NR archery either-sex tag: ~$420.
- NR archery antlerless tag: ~$310.
- NR firearm tags: ~$345 (when drawn).
- Application + bonus tag fees on top.

Season structure:
- Archery: October 1 through mid-January (gun-season pauses).
- Firearm: 3-day mid-November season + 4-day early December season (called "first firearm" and "second firearm").
- Muzzleloader: mid-December.
- Late-winter antlerless and CWD seasons: January in qualifying counties.
- Youth and special hunts: various Saturdays.

Top counties (the "Golden Triangle"):
- Pike: most famous; biggest bucks consistently.
- Adams (just north of Pike): nearly identical reputation.
- Brown: similar farm/timber mix.
- Schuyler: emerging trophy reputation.
- Calhoun: peninsula between Mississippi and Illinois rivers; unique, limited access.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- IL is a "tag-out on a giant" destination — hunters who go often pass dozens of 130-150" bucks looking for the 170+ animal.
- The firearm season weekend pushes deer movement statewide; archers within range of public/leased ground often have phenomenal late December archery.
- IDNR has good public-land hunting via state-owned + IRAP (Illinois Recreational Access Program) leased land — start there before assuming you need private leases.
- IL's CWD-positive counties (mostly NW) have late-season sharpshooter and extended hunter opportunity — sometimes overlooked.

Key deadlines:
- NR archery tag sale: typically early August.
- NR firearm lottery: typically July deadline for following year.
- Resident draw application: spring.

How to answer "should I hunt Illinois":
1. For trophy archery whitetail: yes, immediately — buy the tag in August and plan a 10-14 day trip to W-central IL during peak rut.
2. For firearm: long lottery wait for top counties; alternative is to apply for a county adjacent to the famous ones (often lower draw, similar genetics).
3. Lean into IRAP public access if leasing private isn't feasible.
4. Time the trip for early November (rut) for archery; expect cold and rain or snow.

Guardrails:
- Don't promise a specific buck class — IL produces giants but isn't a guarantee.
- Verify current NR firearm allocation and county availability — quotas shift annually.
- CWD-zone rules affect carcass transport; flag this for hunters planning multi-state trips.
- The famous counties are heavily leased; manage expectations for hunters without connections.
