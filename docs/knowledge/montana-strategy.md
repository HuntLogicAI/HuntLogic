Use this pack for Montana big game strategy, NR combo licensing, special permit applications, or "should I hunt MT" questions.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- MT FWP Hunt portal lists current license structure.
- Annual Big Game Regulations (published spring) is the canonical year-by-year reference.
- FWP Seasons & Applications page is the source of truth for deadlines.

License structure — this is what makes MT different:
- For nonresidents, the entry point is the Big Game Combination License (~$1,000): includes elk + deer + small game + fishing.
- 17,000 NR combo licenses available per year, allocated via a March 1 deadline draw — historically these sell out instantly and the draw is competitive.
- Big game combo gives access to general-season elk and deer in most of the state (where general season applies).
- Residents pay ~$70 for a similar combo — order of magnitude price gap.
- A separate Special Permit application (April 1 deadline) is required for limited-quota hunts within the general framework.

Point system:
- MT uses bonus points (squared in some species, not all) for limited-quota special permits.
- Bonus points apply to: sheep, moose, goat, bison, antelope, and some elk/deer special permits.
- General-season elk and deer DON'T require points — the combo or general license gets you in.
- The point system is less central to MT planning than to UT/NV/WY because general season is the main path.

Top opportunities:
- General-season elk: most of western and central MT is open via general license + combo. Massive country, good public access via Block Management (BMA).
- Mule deer: general season throughout much of the state. Quality has been compressed by recent winterkill in eastern districts — verify current herd status.
- Whitetail: very strong populations in eastern MT, available on combo.
- Special permits for trophy units (Missouri Breaks bull elk, certain mule deer districts): 5-15+ bonus points.
- Sheep / moose / goat: 15-25+ years, lifetime tags.
- Antelope: 0-5 points statewide, declining tag numbers due to winterkill.

Point creep reality:
- Missouri Breaks (units 410, 417, 621, etc.): premier bull elk units, 8-15+ bonus points NR.
- Bitterroot Valley elk: general season opportunity, no points needed.
- Madison Valley: famous for trophy elk, mix of general and permit hunts.
- Sheep: 20-30+ years for nonresidents on quality units.

Key deadlines (verify current year on FWP):
- NR Big Game Combo application: March 1 (this is the gateway — miss it and you can't hunt the general season at all).
- Special Permit application (sheep, moose, goat, limited elk/deer): April 1.
- Draw results for combo: late March / early April.
- Draw results for special permits: late spring.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- Block Management (BMA) is the most valuable hunter-access program in the west — landowners are paid by FWP to allow public access to private land. Hundreds of thousands of acres available. This is the differentiator that makes MT a hunt-every-year destination.
- The Missouri River Breaks is the most famous bull elk country in MT — and one of the most coveted in the west. Worth applying every year once you have any bonus points.
- "Big sky" general season is genuine: you can hunt for two-plus weeks across multiple districts on a single combo license.
- MT weather and access in late November (rifle season) is unforgiving. Plan for snow, mud, and short days.
- Recent eastern MT winterkill (2022-23) has compressed mule deer and antelope quotas — flag this when recommending those species.

How to answer "should I hunt Montana":
1. The NR combo is the single decision that gates everything. If they can commit by March 1, MT is one of the best general-season hunts in the west.
2. For trophy elk hunters: apply for combo + Missouri Breaks special permits every year. Hunt general season while building points.
3. For meat/freezer hunters: general elk + cow tags + BMA access = consistent annual harvest opportunity.
4. For trophy mule deer hunters: be honest about current herd quality — recent winterkill matters.
5. Don't recommend MT to a hunter who needs flexibility on application timing — the March 1 combo deadline is unforgiving.

Guardrails:
- Don't suggest hunting MT general season without the combo license — there is no other NR path.
- Don't quote specific NR combo draw odds without retrieved data; allocation patterns shift year to year.
- Always mention BMA when discussing public-land access — it's the killer feature.
- Verify current grizzly bear distribution and regulations before recommending any backcountry hunt in western MT — grizzly is a non-trivial logistics factor.
