Use this pack for Colorado big game strategy, OTC vs draw decisions, archery elk planning, or "I have X preference points, what can I do" questions.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- CPW Big Game page lists current OTC vs limited-draw structure, GMU boundaries, and hunt codes.
- CPW Preference Points page is the source of truth for point requirements by hunt code.
- Annual big game brochure (PDF) is the canonical reference for any given year — published in March.

Point system:
- Pure preference points (NOT bonus). No squaring. One point = one rank in line.
- Lifetime points: they don't expire and don't reset until you draw a limited tag with them (or transfer to a youth).
- $50-ish per species per year to buy a point if you don't apply.
- 20% nonresident cap on tags in any unit that requires 6+ points to draw (the "high-demand" rule).

What makes Colorado different from WY/UT/NV:
- OTC archery elk in many units — no draw, no point, no fee beyond the license. This is the single most accessible western elk opportunity.
- OTC second/third rifle bull elk in many units (varies year to year, and contracting fast — verify current map).
- Cow elk tags via secondary draw or leftover sale — often available without points and a guaranteed-harvest opportunity for fill-the-freezer hunters.
- The "hybrid draw" approach: leftover and reissue tags drop in August every year, and many quality cow + management tags can be picked up that way.

Point creep — by category:
- Trophy bull units (e.g. units 1, 2, 201, 61): 15-25+ preference points for nonresidents. Multi-decade hauls.
- "Quality" mid-tier bull units: 5-12 points NR. Achievable in 5-10 years from a standing start.
- General-season cow elk: 0-2 points in most units.
- Mule deer in trophy units (Bears Ears, Unit 61, Gunnison Basin): 15-20+ points NR.
- Mule deer in lower-tier units: 1-5 points.
- Pronghorn: 0-5 points for most units, 5-10 for top units.
- Sheep / moose / goat: 15-25+ years — lifetime draws.

Key deadlines (verify current year on CPW):
- Primary draw application: early April (typically April 1-5).
- Draw results: late May / early June.
- Leftover list: early August.
- Reissue tags drop on a rolling basis through fall.
- OTC archery / OTC bull elk: license sales open March 1, available all season.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance (label as consensus, not agency fact):
- The "buy a point, hunt OTC archery" strategy is the single highest-value play in Colorado for a hunter with no points: you go hunting every year via OTC archery while building points for a future quality draw.
- Unit 61 mule deer is the most famous trophy unit in CO and remains the benchmark for point requirements.
- Gunnison Basin units (54, 55, 66, etc.) come up repeatedly for trophy mule deer and good bull elk.
- "Bears Ears" (unit 11) is the highest-quality mule deer unit in the state — and the longest point haul.
- Cow elk hunts in OTC archery units are the most consistently overlooked easy-meat opportunity in the west.
- The "second rifle" and "third rifle" elk seasons OTC are quietly being shrunk — what's OTC this year may not be next year. Always verify the current map.

How to answer "I have X preference points, where should I apply":
1. Anchor to their species first (elk vs deer vs antelope vs lottery).
2. For elk with <5 points: pivot to OTC archery and cow tags rather than waiting decades.
3. For elk with 5-12 points: target the mid-tier quality bull units, not the famous trophy units (which will burn the points + still take 5+ more years).
4. For elk with 15+ points: serious trophy units are in range — name them.
5. For mule deer at any point level under 15: be honest — the famous units are a long wait. Recommend cow/either-sex options and leftover muley tags.

Guardrails:
- Don't list specific hunt codes without retrieved data — CPW renumbers and re-tiers units regularly.
- Don't conflate "OTC" with "easy" — OTC archery elk has 10-20% success rates; OTC bull is hard work.
- Don't recommend the famous trophy units to low-point hunters as if it's an "apply and you'll get lucky" situation. It isn't.
- Remind hunters to check the 20% NR cap rule — some high-demand units restrict NR allocation regardless of points.
