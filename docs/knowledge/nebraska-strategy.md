Use this pack for Nebraska whitetail, mule deer, pronghorn, or elk strategy. NE is one of the most NR-friendly Midwestern states.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- Nebraska Game and Parks Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual Big Game Guide is the canonical reference.
- NE Open Fields and Waters (OFW) program lists available public-access private land.

Why Nebraska matters:
- OTC deer tags for NR in most units — no lottery for whitetail or mule deer in most areas.
- Mule deer in Pine Ridge (NW NE) and W panhandle is genuinely good.
- Pronghorn lottery accessible (0-3 points typical for most units).
- Elk lottery (limited but a real opportunity for NR).
- OFW (Open Fields and Waters) provides large public access to private land.

Tag structure:
- NR deer tag (either whitetail or mule deer, unit-specific): ~$255.
- NR antlerless: ~$95.
- NR pronghorn tag (when drawn): ~$285.
- Habitat stamp + game bird stamp on top.

Season structure:
- Archery: mid-September through end of December.
- Muzzleloader: mid-December into January (statewide).
- Firearm: mid-November (~9-day season).
- Late antlerless seasons in many management units.

Top regions:
- Pine Ridge (NW NE — Dawes, Sioux, Box Butte counties): mule deer country, breaks and ponderosa pine.
- Sand Hills (central NE): unique grass-covered dunes, mule deer + whitetail, pronghorn.
- Republican River bottom (S-central NE): top-tier whitetail genetics.
- Niobrara River corridor: river-bottom whitetail with mule deer overlap.

Mule deer specifically:
- NE mule deer hunting is a NR-friendly path for hunters who can't draw WY/CO/UT tags.
- Pine Ridge units are the trophy zones.
- OTC for many; lottery for some quality units.
- Mature 160-180" mule deer harvested annually.

Pronghorn:
- Limited tags via lottery — most NR units take 0-3 preference points.
- W panhandle and Sandhills are the herds.
- Affordable alternative to WY pronghorn.

Elk:
- NE has a managed elk herd in Pine Ridge area.
- Tiny annual draw, NR-eligible but low odds.
- Don't recommend NE elk as primary strategy.

Sheep:
- NE has reintroduced bighorn sheep; very limited lottery (often resident-only).

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- Pine Ridge in late November (rut) is the highest-leverage mule deer window.
- OFW access can transform a NE trip — book lodging near OFW concentration counties.
- Republican River bottom whitetail is the underrated trophy whitetail opportunity in NE.
- Nebraska is a "show up and hunt" state in the mold of OH/IN — OTC accessibility + decent genetics + lower prices.

Key deadlines:
- NR deer tags: rolling sale (no draw needed for most units).
- Pronghorn lottery: typically March/April.
- Elk lottery: typically March/April.
- Spring turkey: rolling sale OTC.

How to answer "should I hunt Nebraska":
1. Yes for accessible mule deer — Pine Ridge OTC is one of the most reasonable mule deer opportunities in the country.
2. Whitetail in Republican River country is underrated trophy hunting.
3. Pronghorn lottery is achievable — apply every year for cheap points.
4. Don't lead with elk — too few tags for a primary strategy.

Guardrails:
- Verify current OTC vs lottery status by unit — some units shift annually.
- Pine Ridge access is a mix of public (Oglala National Grassland) and private — leverage OFW.
- Don't promise mule deer trophy quality — Pine Ridge produces giants but isn't a lock.
- Some units restrict NR allocation; verify current rules.
