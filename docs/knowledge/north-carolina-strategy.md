Use this pack for North Carolina deer, bear, turkey, or hog strategy. NC has the BIGGEST bears east of the Mississippi.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- NC Wildlife Resources Commission Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual NC Inland Fishing, Hunting and Trapping Regulations is the canonical reference.

Why North Carolina matters:
- Eastern NC (coastal counties: Hyde, Tyrrell, Dare, Washington, Beaufort) produces some of the largest black bears in North America — 600+ lb bears common, 700-800+ lb monsters not unheard of.
- Mountain NC bears (Asheville area) are a separate strong population.
- Strong WMA + Game Land + USFS public hunting.
- Long, generous deer seasons.
- Elk reintroduced in Cataloochee/Great Smoky NP area (no hunting yet).

Tag structure:
- NR season hunting license: ~$125.
- NR big game license: ~$125 add-on (covers deer + bear + wild boar).
- Bear management hunting card: $11 separate.
- Short-term licenses available.

Season structure:
- Archery: early September through end of November (varies by region).
- Blackpowder: October-November.
- Gun: late October through end of December/January (regional).
- Spring turkey: April.
- Bear: late November through early January in coastal zones; varies in mountains.
- Hog: year-round on private + many WMAs.

Bear:
- NC coastal bear is the biggest bear hunting opportunity east of the Mississippi.
- Hounds + still-hunt both legal in many zones.
- Bait NOT legal for bear in NC.
- Most coastal bear hunting is guided due to private land + swampy access.
- Mountain bear (Pisgah, Nantahala NFs) is DIY-accessible.

Top regions:
- Coastal NC: monster bears, deep south swamp country.
- Piedmont (central): farmland deer + suburban edge.
- Mountains (west NC): Pisgah + Nantahala NFs, bear + deer combo.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- NC coastal bear is a destination NR hunt — guided trips $3,000-6,000 typical for 80%+ success on 400+ lb bears.
- Hyde County is THE coastal bear county; multiple longtime guide operations.
- Mountain NC bear via DIY in Pisgah/Nantahala is real — backcountry feel, no bait, hound-only or still-hunt.
- The Cataloochee elk herd is grown for wildlife viewing — not currently huntable.

Key deadlines:
- Bear management hunting card: typically by season start.
- General licenses: rolling sale.

How to answer "should I hunt North Carolina":
1. For bear: yes — coastal NC is THE eastern destination for giant bears.
2. For deer + hog combo: viable, especially eastern WMAs.
3. For mountain bear DIY: USFS public land in west NC works.
4. Don't recommend elk hunting — not currently open.

Guardrails:
- No baiting for bear — flag this for ME/WI hunters used to it.
- Coastal guided hunts book up 12-18 months in advance for prime December.
- Verify current bear zone boundaries and method legality.
- Cataloochee elk = wildlife viewing only, not hunting.
