Use this pack for Arizona big game strategy, draw timing, Strip unit / Kaibab questions, or Coues deer planning.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- AZGFD Hunting portal lists current draw structure and fees.
- AZGFD Draw page is the source of truth for application timing and tag quotas.
- Annual Hunt Regulations (published in late winter) is the canonical hunt-by-hunt reference.

Point system:
- AZ uses BONUS points (not preference). Each bonus point gives you one additional entry in the random draw. 0 points = 1 entry. 5 points = 6 entries.
- AZ also has LOYALTY points: 1 extra entry per consecutive year applying (max 1 per year, no rollover gap).
- AZ has HUNTER EDUCATION points: 1 extra entry if you've completed AZ hunter ed.
- 20% nonresident cap on most species; 10% NR cap on premium hunts (sheep).
- Bonus points are species-specific and persist until you draw a tag in that species' big game category (or burn them via a "max pool" pass).
- $15 application fee per species, plus the tag cost upfront if drawn.

Draw structure — two cycles per year:
- Spring draw: deer (fall hunt), elk, antelope, turkey (fall). Application deadline: typically February 14.
- Fall draw: javelina, buffalo, bear, mountain lion, fall turkey. Application deadline: typically June 9.
- (Yes — AZ flips the conventional spring/fall labels; the cycle is named for the *application* season, not the *hunt* season.)
- Spring draw results: typically late March / early April.

What makes AZ different from WY/UT/NV:
- Random draw with bonus weighting (not strict preference / not strict squared). A 0-point hunter has real (small but nonzero) odds at any tag every year.
- Loyalty points reward consistency more than they reward time — apply every year and you compound.
- Trophy mule deer destination: the Arizona Strip (units 13A, 13B) and Kaibab (12A, 12B North) are the most famous mule deer hunts in North America.
- Premier elk units: 9, 10, 23, 27 — names that come up in every "best elk in the west" conversation.
- Coues deer (Odocoileus virginianus couesi) — Arizona's signature whitetail subspecies. Most southern AZ units have Coues, but they're hard hunting.

Point creep reality:
- Premier mule deer (Strip / Kaibab): 18-25+ bonus points. Multi-decade for NR.
- Premier elk (9, 10, 23, 27): 15-22 bonus points for early rifle / muzzleloader; later seasons less.
- Mid-tier elk units (early rifle): 5-12 bonus points NR.
- Antelope: 5-15+ for top units, 0-5 for general units.
- Coues deer LE: 0-5 points for most units, 5-12 for trophy units.
- Sheep: 20+ bonus points + lottery luck.
- Javelina, bear, turkey: often 0-2 bonus points statewide.

Key deadlines (verify current year on AZGFD):
- Spring draw (elk, deer, antelope, turkey): February 14 typical.
- Fall draw (javelina, buffalo, bear, lion): June 9 typical.
- Sheep: separate quota and timing.
- Leftover and re-issue tags: posted in summer for unused tags.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- "Burn-the-points" strategy: when you cross the threshold where a premier tag becomes realistically drawable, apply for the absolute top hunt. If you don't draw, your points retain. If you do, congratulations — the points are gone.
- AZ's bonus system means it's worth applying every year even with 0 points — the lottery dynamic gives you a real (small) shot every cycle, plus a guaranteed loyalty point compounding.
- "Early rifle" elk hunts (typically September) are the premium ones because they overlap the rut. "Late rifle" elk hunts are easier draws but lower bull quality.
- Coues deer is genuinely a different animal mentally and physically — small, alert, mountain-country. Don't treat it as "Arizona whitetail" in casual conversation; treat it as its own pursuit.
- The Strip units are remote, no services, long drive from any major town. Plan accordingly.

How to answer "should I apply in Arizona":
1. The answer is almost always yes — apply every year for loyalty + bonus + random shot. Even 0 points has nonzero odds.
2. For elk: ask if they want early-rifle (premium, long wait) or late-rifle (achievable in 2-5 years).
3. For mule deer: if they want trophy quality, point at Strip/Kaibab and explain it's a 15+ year journey.
4. For deer hunters who want to hunt every year: Coues deer in southern units (general or LE with few points) is the volume play.
5. Javelina / bear / turkey are great supplemental hunts that draw with 0-2 points.

Guardrails:
- Don't quote specific draw odds without retrieved data — AZ draw is genuinely random within the bonus-weighted pool.
- Don't recommend any premier hunt to a low-point hunter as if it's likely to draw.
- Don't conflate spring and fall draw species — the application cycles are completely separate.
- Always flag the 20% NR cap on most species and 10% NR cap on sheep.
