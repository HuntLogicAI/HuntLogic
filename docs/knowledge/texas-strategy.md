Use this pack for Texas hunting — it's the most complex US state for hunting because of private-land dominance + 11 huntable species + year-round opportunities.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- Texas Parks and Wildlife Department (TPWD) Hunt portal lists current season structure.
- Annual Outdoor Annual (PDF + web) is the canonical reference for seasons, bag limits, regulations.
- TPWD Public Hunts page lists state-managed public hunting opportunities (mostly drawn).

Why Texas matters — the complete picture:
- 11+ huntable big game and exotic species — more than any other US state.
- 97% privately owned land — access is the dominant variable, not regulations.
- Year-round wild hog hunting on private land (no closed season, no bag limit).
- Free-range exotics (axis, aoudad, blackbuck, sika, fallow) huntable year-round on private land.
- Massive whitetail program — TX harvests more whitetail than any other state.
- Mule deer in Trans-Pecos + Panhandle.
- Three turkey subspecies (Rio Grande, Eastern, Merriam's).
- Strong dove + waterfowl + quail (separate pack territory).
- Alligator hunting in coastal counties.

Land access — the most important variable:
- Public land: ~1.5% of TX, mostly via TPWD WMAs, USFS national forests (Sam Houston, Davy Crockett, Angelina, Sabine), Big Bend NP/State Park areas.
- Most public deer hunting on WMAs is via drawn hunts (the Big Time Texas Hunts lottery and Public Hunting Permit drawings).
- Private land access: leases ($1,500-$15,000+/year for deer leases depending on region), day hunts, full-service ranches.
- Some TPWD public lands have annual public hunting permit ($48) — flexible access to designated lands.

Managed Lands Deer (MLD) program:
- Private ranches enrolled in MLD get expanded seasons, larger bag limits, antlerless harvest goals.
- MLD ranches often offer extended deer seasons (October through end of February) for whitetail.
- High-fence vs low-fence ranches: significant cultural and ethical distinction; both legal but discussed differently.

Tag structure:
- NR hunting license: ~$315 (covers everything except some exotics + MLD-specific).
- 5-day NR special license: ~$48.
- Public hunting permit: ~$48 (allows access to most TPWD WMAs).
- Drawn hunt application: ~$3-10 per application.

Season structure (varies by zone):
- Whitetail: archery (late September-early November), general gun (early November to mid-January), muzzleloader + youth seasons.
- Mule deer: late November through mid-December (West TX).
- Javelina: year-round in some zones; specific seasons in others.
- Hog: year-round on private; check WMA-specific rules for public.
- Spring turkey: late March through mid-May.
- Exotics: year-round on private land (no closed season).
- Alligator: late September-October in coastal counties.
- Dove: September-November (3 zones with different opening dates).

Whitetail by region:
- South Texas brush country (Webb, Duval, Dimmit, La Salle counties): legendary trophy zone, 170-200+ inch bucks possible, heavily leased.
- Hill Country (central TX — Edwards Plateau): high deer density, smaller-bodied bucks but high success rates.
- East Texas (Pineywoods): mixed deer + hog, more accessible public land.
- Cross Timbers (N central): mid-tier whitetail.
- Trans-Pecos (W TX): mule deer + javelina + aoudad combo.
- Panhandle: mule deer + whitetail overlap.

Exotic species (year-round, private land):
- Axis deer: high-quality table fare, "venison of choice," common in Hill Country.
- Aoudad (Barbary sheep): free-range in Trans-Pecos mountains, considered top mountain hunt in TX.
- Blackbuck antelope: small but unique, Hill Country ranches.
- Sika deer: smaller introduced population, similar Hill Country ranches.
- Fallow deer: introduced widely, common on commercial hunting ranches.
- Many other exotics: Scimitar oryx, addax, wildebeest, etc. — limited free-range, mostly high-fence trophy ranches.

Hogs:
- Texas has the largest wild hog population in the US (~2-3 million).
- Year-round, no bag limit, no closed season on private land.
- Helicopter hog hunting is legal and a popular eradication method.
- Night hunting with thermal/night vision legal in some configurations.

Turkey:
- Rio Grande turkey: dominant subspecies, Hill Country + South TX.
- Eastern turkey: East TX pineywoods, separate spring quota.
- Merriam's: panhandle, very limited.

Drawn public hunts:
- TPWD "Big Time Texas Hunts" lottery — ~10 packages annually for ultra-premium hunts.
- Public hunt drawings on individual WMAs for specific dates + species.
- Mid-tier success rates; affordable application fees.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- South Texas trophy whitetail is private-land dominated — expect $5K-$15K+ ranch leases for genuinely trophy potential.
- Hill Country day-hunts on small ranches are an accessible NR entry point ($300-$1,500 for a 2-3 day weekend whitetail/exotic combo).
- Aoudad in Trans-Pecos is the most respected free-range exotic in TX — mountain hunt, real challenge.
- Helicopter hog hunts are aggressive eradication trips ($500-$1,500/hour) — controversial but legal.
- The "Texas Slam" (whitetail + mule deer + javelina + turkey + hog) is a recognized achievement.
- Dove season opening day is a Texas cultural event (3 zones, different dates).

Key deadlines:
- General licenses: rolling sale.
- TPWD drawn hunts: typically August/September applications.
- Big Time Texas Hunts: typically October deadline.

How to answer "should I hunt Texas":
1. First question: do you have/can you afford private land access? If yes, TX is uncapped opportunity. If no, focus on TPWD drawn hunts + public hunting permit.
2. For trophy whitetail: South TX brush country with a quality lease, expect significant investment.
3. For affordable diversity: Hill Country day hunts with whitetail + exotic + hog combo.
4. For exotics: aoudad in Trans-Pecos is the most respected hunt.
5. For hog: year-round, generous limits, helicopter as the apex meta.
6. For turkey: Rio Grande in Hill Country in March-April is high-success.
7. For dove: opening day in any of the 3 zones is a cultural experience.

Guardrails:
- TX is 97% private — always frame public-vs-private as the binary decision.
- High-fence vs low-fence is an ethical/cultural distinction; respect hunter preference.
- Exotic species year-round seasons are private-land only — flag this.
- Helicopter hog hunting requires specific licensing and accredited operators.
- Verify regional season dates — TX has multiple zones with different season opens.
- South TX trophy hunting cost expectations should be honest — not a $500 weekend.
