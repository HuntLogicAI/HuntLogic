Use this pack for Tennessee deer, bear, turkey, or hog strategy.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- TN TWRA Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual TN Hunting & Trapping Guide is the canonical reference.

Why Tennessee matters:
- Strong black bear hunting in E TN mountains (Cherokee National Forest, Great Smoky NP edges).
- Year-round wild hog hunting on private land and many WMAs.
- Big WMA system + Cherokee NF + Big South Fork NRRA = strong public hunting.
- Decent whitetail throughout state, no destination-tier trophy zones.
- Small elk lottery (E TN reintroduction).

Tag structure:
- NR hunting license: ~$306 (sportsman bundle covers most species).
- NR small game + big game gun: separate options for shorter trips.
- Bear license: separate purchase.
- Spring turkey permit: included with hunting license.

Season structure:
- Archery: late September through end of January.
- Muzzleloader: early November (~10 days).
- Modern gun: mid-November through early January (with brief pauses).
- Spring turkey: April-May.
- Bear: archery + dog season + modern gun, varies by zone.
- Hog: year-round on most private + some WMAs.

Bear:
- TN E mountain bear hunting is strong — ~600-800 annual harvest.
- Hounding legal in many zones; still-hunt elsewhere.
- Cherokee NF + Big South Fork = primary public-land bear country.

Elk:
- E TN elk reintroduction in N. Cumberland WMA + surrounding counties.
- Tiny lottery (~10-15 NR tags); apply every year.
- Don't recommend as primary strategy.

Hogs:
- Wild hog populations widespread; year-round on private land.
- Many WMAs offer hog hunting concurrent with other seasons.
- Free or low-cost tags; aggressive eradication policy.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- E TN mountain bear hunting is genuinely under-publicized vs western states — strong opportunity for hunters who want a real DIY bear hunt without ME-level travel.
- Cherokee NF supports multi-day backcountry hunting.
- Hog hunting is year-round and unlimited in many areas — meat-grade opportunity.
- Whitetail is solid but not destination-tier; better PA/OH/KY options for trophy.

Key deadlines:
- General licenses: rolling sale.
- Elk lottery: typically June.

How to answer "should I hunt Tennessee":
1. For bear: yes — E TN mountains, Cherokee NF, viable NR DIY.
2. For hog: yes year-round, especially as a supplemental trip.
3. For deer: solid but secondary to KY/OH/MO/PA for trophy.
4. For elk: apply for the lottery but don't expect to draw.

Guardrails:
- Verify bear hound season eligibility by zone.
- Hog hunting WMA-specific rules vary; verify the specific WMA.
- Don't oversell elk — very small program.
