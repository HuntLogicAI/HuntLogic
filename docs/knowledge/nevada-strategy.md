Use this pack for Nevada big game strategy, draw odds reality, or "I have X bonus points in NV" questions. NDOW data is also seeded in the structured database — combine with query_hunting_database for unit-specific draw rates.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- NDOW Hunt portal is the source of truth for current draw structure.
- The annual Big Game Application Booklet (PDF) is the canonical year-by-year reference.
- NDOW Draw Statistics page publishes prior-year draw odds by hunt and bonus point.

Point system:
- Nevada uses a SQUARED bonus point system: entries in the draw = (bonus points² + bonus points + 1). 0 points = 1 entry. 5 points = 31 entries. 10 points = 111 entries. 15 points = 241 entries.
- Identical math to Utah, just an even more extreme squaring effect than preference-based systems.
- 10% nonresident cap on most species (one of the most NR-hostile caps in the west).
- Bonus points are species-specific. Drawing a tag in a species burns those points to zero.
- $14 application fee per species + tag cost upon draw.

What makes NV strategically distinct:
- The 10% NR cap is the binding constraint for nonresidents — even if you have 20 squared bonus points, you're competing in a 10% sliver of the quota.
- Squared math + small NR pools means point creep is severe in trophy units.
- "Partnership" applications: 2 hunters apply together, both must have same point count or draw is based on lowest. Strategy for spouses or hunting partners.
- Alternate tags: if a drawn hunter doesn't pick up their tag, it goes to the alternate list. Worth being on it.
- Resident tag deferment: residents can defer a successful draw to the following year (one-time per species per cycle).

Point creep reality:
- Premium elk (Areas 6, 7, 10, 13): 12-20+ bonus points NR for trophy bull.
- Mid-tier elk: 5-10 points NR.
- General-quality elk: 0-5 points but cap-limited.
- Mule deer in Ruby Mountains (Area 10, 11) and Jarbidge: 10-20+ points for trophy NR.
- Mule deer general units: 0-5 points but with NR cap pressure.
- Pronghorn: 0-10 points; Sheldon NWR (Area 32) is famous for trophy bucks.
- Sheep (California, desert, Rocky Mountain): 15-25+ bonus points + lottery — multi-decade hauls.

Key deadlines (verify current year on NDOW):
- Big game application: late April / early May (typically May 8-12).
- Draw results: typically late June.
- Sheep application: separate, typically earlier in spring.
- Alternates list: published with primary results.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- The squared system + 10% NR cap means a 0-point NR in NV is genuinely unlikely to draw a quality elk tag in any reasonable timeframe.
- "OK not drawing instead of drawing a non-trophy unit" is a valid multi-year strategy for the squared system — every unsuccessful year doubles your effective odds growth on premium hunts.
- NV mule deer in the Ruby Mountains is one of the most coveted trophy mule deer hunts in the west — comparable to UT Henry Mountains and AZ Strip in reputation.
- Sheldon Antelope Refuge (Area 32) for pronghorn is a "go once, hunt forever" reputation tag.
- NV has good cow elk opportunities in some units — often-overlooked meat option with lower point requirements.
- The NV chat history note: at 7 points for elk in NV, you are sub-1% on top units and the "lottery ticket" framing is correct.

How to answer "I have X bonus points in NV":
1. Translate to squared entries: 5 points = 31 entries, 10 points = 111 entries.
2. Apply the 10% NR cap honestly — even good point counts struggle in NR pools.
3. For trophy elk at <10 points: realistic frame is "lottery odds, not expected draw."
4. For trophy mule deer at <10 points: same — Ruby Mountains is a long wait.
5. For pronghorn / sheep / cow tags: more achievable point thresholds; recommend these as parallel plays.
6. Validate the "apply premium every cycle and bank points if not drawn" strategy as sound multi-year math for squared systems.
