Use this pack for New Hampshire moose lottery, bear, or deer strategy. NH has solid moose + bear, modest deer.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- NH Fish and Game Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual NH Hunting Digest is the canonical reference.
- NH F&G publishes moose lottery results and bear/deer harvest data.

Why New Hampshire matters:
- Moose lottery — small but viable (~50-100 permits annually).
- Strong bear hunting in the White Mountains and North Country.
- Modest deer hunting; mostly suburban edge in southern NH.
- Long bear baiting season + hound option in some WMUs.

Moose lottery:
- Application: typically May.
- Small annual quota (~50-100 permits, varies with herd surveys).
- Resident heavily favored in allocation.
- NR draw odds genuinely tiny — apply every year, accept lottery-grade odds.
- Subpermittee allowed.

Bear:
- NH harvests ~600-800 bears annually.
- Bait, hound, and still-hunting all legal in most WMUs.
- Bait season: typically early September through early October.
- Hound season: mid-September through end of October.
- Firearm: mid-September through early December (varies by WMU).

Deer:
- NH deer hunting is modest by national standards — total annual harvest ~10,000-12,000.
- Higher densities in southern NH suburban/farm edge.
- White Mountains region: big country, lower density, traditional rifle hunt.
- Antlerless tags via WMU-specific lottery.

Tag structure:
- NR hunting license: ~$113.
- NR moose application: ~$15 + tag fee ~$580 if drawn.
- NR bear tag: ~$50 add-on.
- Muzzleloader + archery licenses: $26 each.

Season structure:
- Archery: mid-September through mid-December.
- Muzzleloader: late October / early November.
- Firearm: mid-November through early December.
- Youth deer day: October.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- NH moose hunting is best as a lottery applicant — odds are low but the experience is genuine.
- North Country bear over bait is the most reliable NH big game hunt for NR.
- NH suburban deer is undervalued — high density, archery-friendly, multiple antlerless tags in some zones.
- The White Mountains National Forest provides large contiguous public hunting.

Key deadlines:
- Moose lottery: typically May.
- Bear and deer licenses: rolling sale.
- Antlerless lottery (residents): typically August.

How to answer "should I hunt New Hampshire":
1. For moose: apply for the experience and the lottery shot — don't expect to draw.
2. For bear: viable NR destination with guides in North Country.
3. For deer: NH isn't a primary destination but works for suburban-zone archery hunters near southern NH.
4. White Mountains big-country experience is real but low success.

Guardrails:
- Don't oversell NH moose — NR odds are very low.
- Verify current bear method legality by WMU.
- Antlerless allocation for NR depends on resident leftover; verify availability.
