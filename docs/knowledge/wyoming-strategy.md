Use this pack for any Wyoming big game strategy, application timing, point math, or "should I start here" questions.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- WGFD application portal is the source of truth for current-year deadlines, fees, and quotas.
- WGFD draw odds statistics page publishes prior-year draw data by unit, residency, and points.
- Hunting regs document lists Type 1/2/3/4/6/7 license definitions and season structures.

Point system — this is what makes WY complex:
- WY uses a hybrid: 75% of nonresident limited-quota tags go through a "regular" draw (squared preference + bonus mix depending on species), 25% through a "special" draw that costs more per tag but draws faster.
- Preference points apply to elk, deer, antelope, sheep, moose, and goat.
- The max-point pool: if the highest point holder didn't draw a unit last year, the unit "creeps" — meaning your point requirement to draw that unit went up by 1 the next year.
- $52 per species per year to buy a preference point if you don't apply (or apply only for ineligible units).
- Sheep/moose/goat: lifetime tag (one per species, ever). Point creep is brutal — 15-25+ years for top units.

Point creep reality — say this plainly:
- For sheep, moose, and goat: a 0-point nonresident is statistically 15-25 years from a tag in trophy units. This is a multi-decade commitment.
- For elk in famous units (e.g. units 100, 105, area 7): trophy bull units typically require 10-15+ preference points for nonresidents.
- For elk general license (the "region G/H/J/etc." multi-unit licenses): often 0-3 points draws. This is the volume play and the right starting strategy for most nonresidents.
- For mule deer in region G/H (Wyoming Range): historically the gold standard, but heavy winterkill in recent years has compressed draw odds and crashed buck quality. Verify current herd status before recommending.
- For antelope: historically the most accessible western antelope hunt, but 2022-2024 winterkill in central/southern WY cut tag quotas significantly.

Key deadlines (verify current year on WGFD):
- Sheep / moose / goat / bison: late January application deadline (typically Jan 31).
- Elk: early February application deadline (typically Feb 1).
- Deer / antelope: early May application deadline (typically May 31).
- Refund deadlines fall ~2 weeks before the season opens.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance (label clearly as hunter consensus):
- The "first-choice strategy" matters in WY: your first choice is the only one that costs you a point if you don't draw. Use it on your dream unit; lower choices are essentially "free" attempts.
- The "special" draw (higher fee, separate quota) consistently draws with fewer points than the "regular" draw for the same unit — worth it if you can afford the higher cost.
- General region elk is genuinely good — multi-unit hunting, decent bull quality, and you actually go hunting every year. Don't sleep on it because it's not "limited entry."
- Wyoming requires the full license fee up front at application (refunded if unsuccessful for most species). This locks up $700-$1500 of capital for months — relevant for cash flow.
- Bonus points are now sold separately from applications for many species — check the year's rules; this changed recently.

How to answer "should I start applying in Wyoming":
1. Ask first what species and what timeframe. The answer for elk at 5 years is different from sheep at 30 years.
2. If they're 30+ and want a sheep/moose/goat tag in their lifetime, the answer is yes, start now — even a 1-year head start matters when point creep compounds.
3. If they want elk in 5 years, point to general region licenses (achievable) over trophy units (not achievable in 5 years for a 0-pointer).
4. If they want antelope, flag the recent winterkill before recommending — quotas and quality are in flux.
5. Always mention the buy-a-point option for years they can't hunt.

Guardrails:
- Don't quote specific draw rates without retrieved data — quotas shift year to year.
- Don't tell a 0-point hunter they'll draw a top elk unit in under 8 years (they won't).
- Don't conflate the "regular" and "special" draws — they're separate quota pools with different fee structures.
- Always recommend verifying current-year fees and deadlines on WGFD — Wyoming changes mechanics more often than most western states.
