Use this pack for Delaware deer or turkey strategy. DE is the second-smallest state with a small hunt program.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- DE DNREC Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual DE Hunting Guide is the canonical reference.

Why Delaware matters:
- Very high deer density in some zones — Sussex County is a quiet trophy zone.
- Multiple antlerless tags available.
- Long archery season + shotgun + muzzleloader stack.

Tag structure:
- NR hunting license: ~$199.
- NR Quality Buck tag: ~$60.
- Antlerless tags: rolling sale.

Season structure:
- Archery: September through end of January (very long).
- Shotgun: November and January (multiple short windows).
- Muzzleloader: October and January.
- Spring turkey: April.

Notes:
- No rifle for deer — shotgun + muzzleloader + archery only.
- Strong DE Quality Buck (DQB) program: $60 tag, second buck only, with 4-point antler restriction.
- Multiple antlerless tags ($10 each) plus bonus tags in some zones.

Top regions:
- Sussex County (S DE): high-density, mature buck potential.
- Kent County (central): moderate density, more public access.
- New Castle County (N DE): heavily suburban, limited hunting.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- DE is genuinely overlooked by trophy hunters — Sussex County produces mature bucks.
- The DQB program shows DE is serious about quality management.
- Sussex County leases trade at moderate prices vs PA/MD.
- Long archery + abundant antlerless = excellent meat-yield potential.

Key deadlines:
- Licenses: rolling sale.

How to answer "should I hunt Delaware":
1. For Eastern Seaboard residents: yes — manageable trip with real trophy potential in Sussex.
2. For destination hunters: niche but viable, especially with the DQB tag.
3. Combo trip with MD sika hunting works well geographically.

Guardrails:
- No rifle hunting.
- Verify zone-specific antlerless allocation.
- The DQB tag has antler-point restriction; flag the rule.
