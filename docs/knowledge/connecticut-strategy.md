Use this pack for Connecticut deer, bear, or turkey strategy. CT is a small suburban-heavy hunt state with growing bear opportunity.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- CT DEEP Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual CT Hunting Guide is the canonical reference.

Why CT matters:
- High suburban deer density in many zones — extended seasons + multiple antlerless.
- Bear hunting recently legalized in some zones — small but growing program.
- Long archery + short shotgun + muzzleloader stack.

Tag structure:
- NR firearms license: ~$153.
- NR archery license: ~$117.
- Antlerless permits via zone-specific lottery.

Season structure:
- Archery: mid-September through end of January (very long).
- Shotgun: mid-November through mid-December (~3 weeks).
- Muzzleloader: mid-December.
- Bear: limited season in qualifying zones, recently introduced.

Notes:
- CT bans rifle for deer — shotgun + muzzleloader + archery only.
- Bear hunting recently legalized in NW zones; verify current rules.
- Strong WMA (Wildlife Management Area) system.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- CT deer hunting is overlooked by destination hunters — high density + accessible suburban hunts.
- The new bear hunt is small but a real opportunity.
- Long archery season + suburban + multiple antlerless = high meat-yield hunt for in-state hunters.

Key deadlines:
- Antlerless lottery: typically August.
- General license: rolling sale.

How to answer "should I hunt Connecticut":
1. For nearby residents: yes — productive suburban deer hunting.
2. For destination hunters: probably no.
3. For bear: track the program — new and small but expanding.

Guardrails:
- No rifle hunting for deer.
- Verify current bear zone eligibility — new program with evolving rules.
- WMA hunting often requires specific permits beyond general license.
