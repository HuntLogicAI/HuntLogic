Use this pack for Michigan deer, bear, or elk strategy. UP vs LP is the first question.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- MI DNR Hunting portal lists current season structure.
- Annual Hunting Digest (PDF) is the canonical reference.
- DNR Elk and Bear application pages list current point thresholds.

Deer landscape — UP vs LP:
- Upper Peninsula (UP): big-woods deer hunting, lower density (5-15 per square mile), severe winter mortality, smaller bucks on average. Cultural rifle hunt with deep tradition.
- Northern Lower Peninsula (NLP): mixed forest/farm, moderate density, average bucks.
- Southern Lower Peninsula (SLP): high-density farmland whitetail, increasing baiting bans due to CWD, biggest mature bucks in MI.
- APR (Antler Point Restriction) zones: some DMUs require 4 points on one side; verify the current map.

Tag structure:
- Resident deer license + buck tag bundled in base license; combo license adds antlerless and second buck.
- NR deer license: ~$170.
- Bonus antlerless tags: vary by DMU and year, sometimes very generous, sometimes capped.
- Bait/feed regulations vary widely by CWD zone — UP largely OK, much of SLP banned.

Season structure:
- Archery: October 1 through mid-November and then late November through January 1 (gun-season gap).
- Firearm: November 15-30 (statewide gun season).
- Muzzleloader: early December (zone-dependent).
- Late antlerless and urban-zone seasons: various extensions.

Bear landscape:
- MI has a strong bear program — largest population east of the Mississippi outside MN.
- 9 bear hunting units, mostly in the UP and northern LP.
- Lottery with preference points. Top UP units (Bergland, Carney) take 5-12+ points NR.
- Bait + hounds legal in most zones (with hound restrictions in some).
- ~$15 application fee + tag cost upon draw.

Elk landscape:
- MI has a small managed elk herd in the Pigeon River State Forest (NE LP).
- ~200-300 tags issued annually by lottery; preference point system.
- Top hunters wait 10-20+ years for a bull tag.
- Hunt # and zone determine quality — fall hunt period 1 is the premier hunt.
- Apply every year — points compound and the herd is well-managed.

Turkey:
- Spring lottery + leftover sale. Most applicants get a tag.
- Fall turkey limited to certain zones.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- UP deer hunting is a generational tradition (deer camps, gun-season opening day) more than a quality-buck destination.
- The Pigeon River elk herd is genuinely huntable — different feel than western hunts (smaller country, more managed).
- Bait bans are a moving target — verify by county before planning.
- Drummond Island (Lake Huron) is a unique island whitetail hunt with limited access via ferry.
- The southern LP produces real trophy whitetails but baiting restrictions force more traditional stand-hunting.

Key deadlines:
- Elk application: typically May 1.
- Bear application: typically May 1.
- Turkey application: typically early February.
- Deer licenses: rolling sale.

How to answer "should I hunt Michigan":
1. UP for the cultural deer-camp experience; manage expectations on buck quality.
2. SLP farmland for trophy potential, but expect access challenges.
3. Bear in UP — start point-building now, plan for 5-12 year wait in top units.
4. Elk in Pigeon River — apply every year, accept multi-decade lottery.
5. Spring turkey is easy and casual.

Guardrails:
- Verify current bait ban map — it changes annually with CWD detections.
- UP success rates are honest 10-20% on bucks; don't oversell.
- Pigeon River elk is small-country hunting, not western backcountry.
- APR zones change; verify current rules.
