Use this pack for Alaska big game strategy. AK is structurally different from every other US state — assume nothing.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- Alaska Dept of Fish and Game (ADF&G) Hunting portal is the source of truth.
- Annual AK Hunting Regulations is the canonical reference; broken into multiple regional booklets.
- ADF&G drawing permits page lists current draw odds.

Why Alaska is structurally different:
- 31 GMUs (Game Management Units), each with its own regulations, sometimes broken into subunits.
- 5 distinct ecosystems: Southeast (Tongass NF, Sitka deer + black bear), South-central (moose + caribou + brown bear access via Anchorage), Interior (moose + grizzly + sheep), Western (caribou herds + tundra moose), Arctic (Brooks Range sheep + caribou + muskox).
- Most hunts require fly-out access; logistics dominate planning.
- Bush plane charters $1,500-$3,500+ per round trip.
- Float trips, raft hunts, cabin-based hunts all common.
- Weather is the dominant operational variable.

NR guide requirement — critical:
- NR hunters MUST be accompanied by a registered AK guide or Alaska resident 2nd-degree relative for:
  - Dall sheep
  - Brown bear / grizzly bear
  - Mountain goat
- DIY NR hunting is allowed for: moose, caribou, black bear, deer (Sitka blacktail), wolf.
- Guide costs: $15K-$40K+ for sheep/brown bear; $8K-$20K for goat.

Tag structure:
- NR hunting license: ~$160.
- NR big game tags by species:
  - Moose: ~$800 (locking-tag system, often unit-specific)
  - Caribou: ~$650
  - Brown/grizzly bear: ~$1,000 (huntable with registered guide)
  - Black bear: ~$450
  - Dall sheep: ~$850 (with guide)
  - Mountain goat: ~$600 (with guide)
  - Sitka blacktail: ~$300

Top species + hunts:
- **Dall sheep**: Brooks Range + Wrangell-St. Elias + Chugach + Tok-area. Premier mountain hunt in NA. Requires registered guide for NR. $20K-$40K typical guided hunt.
- **Brown bear**: Coastal AK (Kodiak, Katmai NP fringe, Alaska Peninsula) — biggest bears in the world. Requires registered guide for NR. $15K-$35K guided hunt.
- **Moose**: Most populous big game in AK. DIY-accessible via fly-out. Western AK (Kuskokwim drainage), Interior (Yukon River drainage), South-central (Susitna River). $4K-$12K DIY-with-charter; $8K-$20K guided.
- **Caribou**: 4 main herds (Western Arctic, Porcupine, Central Arctic, Mulchatna). DIY accessible. Migration timing is the meta. $4K-$8K typical DIY trip.
- **Sitka blacktail**: Smaller body (~75-100 lbs), Southeast AK (Tongass) + Kodiak + Afognak. DIY-friendly, skiff or float access. Long seasons.
- **Mountain goat**: Coastal mountains + Kenai + Kodiak. With guide for NR. Limited entry.
- **Muskox**: Limited drawing permits, Arctic + Nunivak Island. Multi-year wait.
- **Bison**: Tiny herds, Copper River + Farewell. Lottery-only.

Permit types:
- Draw permits (DP): annual lottery for many species/units.
- Registration permits (RG): first-come, no quota, often issued in advance.
- Tier I/II subsistence permits: residents preference, sometimes NR ineligible.
- General season: OTC for many DIY-eligible species (moose in many units, caribou in some, blacktail).

Season structure (varies by GMU):
- Most big game: August-October (fall hunts).
- Some early August seasons for caribou herds and early sheep.
- Some winter seasons for black bear in coastal areas.
- Spring brown bear seasons in some coastal units.

Top regions:
- **Southeast AK**: Sitka blacktail + black bear, skiff/float access from Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, Petersburg.
- **South-central AK**: Moose + caribou + black bear access from Anchorage.
- **Interior AK**: Dall sheep + moose + grizzly access from Fairbanks/Tok.
- **Western AK**: Massive caribou herds + moose, fly-in from McGrath/Bethel.
- **Arctic AK**: Brooks Range sheep + Porcupine herd caribou + muskox, fly-in from Fairbanks/Kotzebue.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- DIY moose in Western AK (often "Western Brooks Range" units like 23, 24A, 26) is the most accessible NR DIY big game opportunity — though logistics are real.
- Caribou over the Haul Road (Dalton Highway) is the cheapest AK hunt — drive-in access to GMU 25 + 26.
- Sitka deer DIY in SE AK can be done from a small boat at modest cost (~$3K-$5K all-in for a week).
- Brown bear hunting is genuinely once-in-a-lifetime for most NR — book reputable guide 2-3 years out for prime spring/fall slots.
- Weather can shut down fly-out hunts for days; plan extra time + flexibility.
- Pack-out logistics are real — a bull moose is 600-800 lbs of meat, requires multiple loads.

Key deadlines:
- Draw permit applications: typically October-December for following fall.
- Registration permits: rolling, often available year-round.
- General licenses: rolling sale.

How to answer "should I hunt Alaska":
1. First question: what species + budget? Sheep/brown bear require $15K-$40K+ and a guide. Moose/caribou DIY is $4K-$12K.
2. For DIY: moose or caribou in fly-out Western/Interior units; Sitka deer in Southeast.
3. For guided: dall sheep is the iconic AK hunt; brown bear is the apex.
4. Apply for draw permits every year even if not planning to hunt — points compound for some units.
5. Logistics are the actual hunt — flight charters, weather, pack-out planning.

Guardrails:
- NEVER recommend NR hunting dall sheep, brown bear, or mountain goat without a registered guide — it's illegal.
- DIY moose/caribou requires real logistics planning; don't oversell as casual trips.
- AK weather can ground flights for 3-7 days; plan flexibility.
- Multiple GMUs per region; verify the specific GMU before quoting any rules.
- Some units restrict NR access by season; always verify.
- Predator densities (grizzly, wolf) are real in many backcountry units; bear-aware camp protocols mandatory.
