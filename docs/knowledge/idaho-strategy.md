Use this pack for Idaho big game strategy, NR zone capping mechanics, controlled hunt applications, or "should I start applying in ID" questions.

Tier 1 official anchors:
- IDFG Hunt portal lists current season and tag structure.
- Annual Big Game Seasons & Rules booklet (published February-March) is the canonical reference.
- IDFG license page is the source of truth for current fees and quotas.

Tag structure — this is what makes ID different from every other western state:
- ID has NO bonus or preference point system. Period.
- Most tags are sold on a FIRST-COME-FIRST-SERVED basis on a defined sale date.
- For nonresidents: tags are capped at 10% of available quota (varies by species and zone).
- General-season elk and deer tags for NR are sold by ZONE. Many zones sell out within minutes of going on sale.
- Some hunts require a separate controlled-hunt application (the only "draw" in ID).
- $185 NR elk tag, $355 NR deer tag (approximate, verify current year).

Sale-date strategy — the entire game:
- NR general elk tags go on sale December 1 (annual).
- NR general deer tags go on sale December 1 (annual).
- The popular zones (Sawtooth, Selway-Bitterroot, Lolo, Diamond Creek, Boise River, Owyhee) sell out in 1-15 minutes.
- Less popular zones may have leftover NR quota into spring or even summer.
- Each NR can buy ONE elk tag and ONE deer tag — there's no stockpiling.

Controlled hunt draw:
- The only "true draw" in ID — for limited-quota hunts inside the general framework.
- No bonus points, no preference points — pure random within the applicant pool.
- Application deadline: June 5 (typical) for elk, deer, antelope, plus separate windows for moose/sheep/goat.
- Draw results: mid-July.

Top opportunities:
- Frank Church Wilderness elk (multiple zones overlap): the biggest backcountry public-land elk hunt in the lower 48.
- Selway-Bitterroot: famous wilderness elk; tough country, low success rate, low pressure.
- Owyhee: high-desert mule deer and antelope, great public land, fewer hunters.
- Sawtooth zones: most coveted general elk + deer combo zones — sell out instantly.
- Diamond Creek: popular Wyoming-border elk zone.
- Sheep / moose / goat: lifetime controlled hunts, draw odds 0.5-5% depending on unit.

Capped zone mechanics:
- Once an NR cap is hit in a zone, you cannot buy a tag for that zone — even mid-season.
- Some zones have "either-species" or "rifle/archery" sub-caps within a single zone.
- A few zones split into general + capped categories — verify the specific zone before claiming "OTC."

Key deadlines (verify current year on IDFG):
- Tag sales open (NR general elk/deer): December 1 at 10am MT.
- Controlled hunt application: June 5 typical.
- Sheep / moose / goat draw deadline: April 30 typical.
- Black bear / mountain lion tags: rolling general sale.

Tier 2 hunter-consensus nuance:
- The December 1 sale day is the single most important date in ID hunting. Be online, account set up, payment ready, refresh button at the ready. Popular zones disappear in literal minutes.
- "Backup zone" strategy: have 2-3 zones in mind before sale day. If your first choice is gone, immediately try the second.
- The Frank Church and Selway-Bitterroot are real wilderness hunts — multi-day pack-ins, low success rates (often 10-15%), but world-class scenery and elk country.
- General-season ID elk is a great every-year option for the price — if you can secure the tag.
- The lack of a point system means there's no "long game" — ID is a hunt-every-year-or-don't decision, not a "build points for 10 years" state.

How to answer "should I start applying in ID":
1. Reframe: ID isn't really an "apply" state — it's a "buy a tag fast on sale day" state.
2. For an annual hunting destination: yes, but commit to the December 1 logistics.
3. For controlled hunts (lottery): apply every year for sheep/moose/goat; treat each as a one-shot lottery.
4. For someone who can't commit to a specific December date: ID is not the right state. Recommend OR, WA, or general-season AZ instead.
5. Always frame Frank Church / Selway as "wilderness commitment" hunts, not casual trips.

Guardrails:
- Don't recommend a specific zone without verifying current NR cap status — sale-day inventory changes annually.
- Don't suggest "applying for points in Idaho" — there are none.
- Don't conflate general-season tags (December 1 sale) with controlled-hunt tags (June draw application).
- Always flag that wilderness backcountry hunts in ID are non-trivial — gear, time, pack support, weather.
